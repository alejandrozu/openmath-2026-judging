Executive brief
What this system changes and what it does not claim
Decision  
Replace a single difficulty label with a versioned profile: status gate, intrinsic difficulty, AI-relative difficulty, human effort and exposure, tractability, verification, formalization, prerequisites, tool leverage, uncertainty, and a one-line audit trail for every axis.

The operating principles
Profile, not prestige ranking. A public tier summarizes D, but the other axes remain visible because different users care about different bottlenecks.
Status before difficulty. A problem that is solved, ill-posed, duplicated, or stale cannot become a valid benchmark merely because it received a hard score.
Evidence before certainty. Automated assessments stop at C2; independently reviewed `verified_open` and C3 are deliberately empty in this first pass.
Human and machine difficulty are different questions. AI difficulty is dated to a stated tool-and-scaffolding protocol and must be refreshed as systems change.
Short rationales are the audit trail. Each line states an observable cue, its scoring mechanism, and the material uncertainty; the ledger does not expose hidden chain-of-thought.
How to use the two deliverables
Workbook: 
The complete 5,426-record assessment, formulas, per-axis rationales, source text, QA flags, and editable inputs.
This guide: 
The definitions, scoring rules, calibration protocol, corpus audit, governance controls, and representative examples needed to defend or revise the ledger.
Guardrail  
An opportunity tag is a high-precision inclusion rule, not a complete search result. Absence of a tag is not evidence that a problem is unsuitable.

2. Intrinsic difficulty D
A weighted judgment for a fully prepared specialist or team
Definition  
D is expected difficulty from the current literature for a fully prepared specialist or team using ordinary modern tools. It is not fame, importance, age, or the record's legacy label.
D = round(2.5 x (0.30 CG + 0.20 RG + 0.20 TD + 0.20 KB + 0.10 SS), 1)

3. AI-relative difficulty
A dated machine-side adjustment, never a timeless property
Protocol snapshot  
Frontier generalist agent; retrieval, code/CAS, search, and theorem-prover tools allowed; no unpublished expert oracle; human scaffolding level 1; assessment date 2026-07-31.
AI difficulty = clamp(D + AI relative, 0, 10)
Predictor
Coding rule
Formal fit
-1 when the statement maps cleanly to symbolic/formal objects; +1 when formalization or ambiguity is high.
Verification feedback
-1 when candidate outputs are cheaply and exactly checked; +1 when both proof directions require heavy specialist review.
Tool leverage
-1 when computation, CAS, SAT/SMT, search, or proof assistants supply a tight loop; +1 when tools offer little direct signal.
Context load
-1 for a compact literature footprint; +1 for a large, fragmented, or highly interdependent literature.
Reasoning horizon
-1 for bounded targets; +1 for long-horizon, high-D, or programmatic work.
Tacit / experimental insight
-1 where explicit computation dominates; +1 where geometric, physical, experimental, or field-tacit insight appears central.
Each predictor is coded -1 favorable, 0 mixed, or +1 hostile. Their sum is multiplied by 0.5, giving an AI-relative range of -3 to +3. AI-favored is <= -1; AI-hostile is >= +1; everything between is neutral/mixed.
What this score can and cannot say
It can prioritize problems with exact feedback, compact context, and manageable verification relative to similarly human-rated problems.
It cannot certify that an AI will solve a problem. These are editorial estimates, not benchmark runs, and therefore never exceed C2.
It must be versioned by model, tools, retrieval corpus, budget, human scaffolding, and date. A protocol change creates a new score series.

6. Rationale standard
A reproducible audit trail in one or two lines per axis
Every problem receives separate rationales for D, the five D factors, AI difficulty, tool leverage, H/X, T, verification, formalization, prerequisites, and status/data quality. The target is 18-45 words whenever practical.
Required sentence shape
1
observable cue
2
scoring mechanism
3
uncertainty
Template  
[Score and range]/[confidence] - [Observable statement, source, or metadata fact]; [why that fact changes this axis]; [missing evidence or protocol caveat].
Acceptable evidence
Statement form: exact value, bound, construction, classification, proof/disproof, experimental, or programmatic target.
Scope: atomic, family, compound, or programmatic; compound and programmatic records widen uncertainty or trigger curation.
Background cues: explicit reductions, partial cases, no-known-route language, resistance, references, dates, and current status evidence.
Machine cues: formal fit, exact feedback, tool loop, context load, reasoning horizon, and tacit/experimental burden.
Corpus cues: missing or malformed text, rights/status notes, duplicate signals, source recovery, and fallback provenance.
Prohibited shortcuts
Hard because famous, easy because readable, or important because old.
Treating a legacy collection label as an expert judgment about the individual record.
Presenting AI estimates as empirical performance or human-hour bands as observed labor totals.
Using a problem number as the join key; problem numbers collide across and within collections.
7. First-pass scoring pipeline
Deterministic, source-aware, and explicitly provisional
Normalize and preserve.
 Load the frozen problems.json snapshot, preserve Dataset ID, clean display text without overwriting source content, and recover source/fallback URLs.
Gate and flag.
 Apply catalog status, source review notes, malformed-text checks, duplicate signals, truncation checks, and programmatic-scope rules before opportunity ranking.
Classify the target.
 Detect answer type, scope, mathematical objects, prerequisite category, literature cues, dates, references, and computation/formalization signals.
Score inputs.
 Assign factor inputs, H/X, V, F, P/B, and tool leverage from documented rules; cap automated confidence at C2.
Derive outputs.
 Compute D, tier, AI relative/difficulty, uncertainty ranges, T, route, high-precision tags, review priority, and short per-axis rationales.
Verify and version.
 Run formula and visual QA, record rule version, dataset version, date, and SHA-256, then route exceptions to manual review.
Why this is an editorial first pass
The rule pass reads the supplied corpus; it is not 5,426 independent literature reviews. Its purpose is to replace inconsistent source labels with a transparent baseline, expose uncertainty, and create a review queue that experts can improve without reverse-engineering hidden judgments.

12. Calibration and validation plan
Move from transparent baseline to expert-reviewed measurement
12.1 Gold-set design
Select 120-150 records stratified by source, category, answer type, scope, gate, confidence, and score range; oversample C0/C1 and extreme scores.
Use three independent raters. Blind them to the legacy difficulty label and to each other's scores.
Create anchor cards for every D factor and supporting axis, including counterexamples that separate fame, visibility, effort, and intrinsic difficulty.
Adjudicate all gate disagreements, D disagreements above 1.5 points, and any opportunity tag that depends on a disputed threshold.

12.3 Empirical AI protocol
Freeze model/version, context limit, retrieval corpus, tools, token and wall-clock budget, human scaffolding, and random seeds.
Run at least three independent episodes per calibration problem; log tool calls and artifacts while keeping evaluator access separate.
Score outcomes on a fixed ladder: 0 no progress, 1 useful restatement, 2 checked known result, 3 novel checked partial result, 4 full independently checked resolution.
Calibrate AI relative and T against observed outcome rates; never back-fit D to make model performance look smooth.
12.4 Maintenance cadence
Recalibrate after each 1,000 reviewed records, any major source ingest, or any material AI protocol change. Publish confusion tables, disagreement cases, parameter diffs, and a changelog. Preserve old snapshots so benchmark claims remain reproducible.
13. Limitations and governance
What must be reviewed before this becomes a public benchmark standard
Limitation
Why it matters
Control
Literature status
No independent current-literature audit was performed for every record.
Use source_claimed_open/status_unclear; reserve verified_open for documented review.
Rule-pass bias
Text cues and source metadata cannot recover tacit field knowledge.
Keep C0-C2, wide ranges, and expert override reasons.
Human hours
Unpublished attempts and distributed background work are unobserved.
Treat H as an order-of-magnitude prior, not an observed labor total.
AI estimates
No repeated benchmark episodes were run in this release.
Treat AI/T as protocol-dated forecasts; validate empirically.
Corpus integrity
Malformed, duplicated, context-dependent, and rights/status-review records remain.
Complete P0 curation before public evaluation or redistribution.
Importance
The profile does not score scientific, social, or strategic value.
Add a separate stakeholder-specific impact model if needed; do not overload V.
Manual edits
Some derived presentation fields are not live formulas.
Regenerate the full workbook before release.
Recommended adoption sequence
Adopt OPDP v1.0 as a transparent provisional baseline, not as an expert-certified truth set.
Clear P0 corpus issues and build the 120-150 problem gold set.
Calibrate rater agreement, revise anchor cards, and lock OPDP v1.1 parameters.
Run the frozen AI protocol on the gold set and recalibrate AI relative/T.
Publish reviewed score diffs, reviewer evidence, and stable snapshot identifiers.
