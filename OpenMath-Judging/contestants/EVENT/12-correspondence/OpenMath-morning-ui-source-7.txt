7 verifiers of open math problems curated by Alejandro Zarzuelo Urdiales for the Open Math Challange.

Start here
Register for the event, using the registration link on OpenMath. Coordinate your team of 1–4 with the organizers.
Sign in to AutoLab, choose a runnable hill below, and read its README, scoring direction, and version.
Click Start a climb. For results tracked on AutoLab, choose On AutoLab, select AutoLab's agent or your coding agent, set a model budget, and connect or rent compute before starting. Compute is billed separately.
Inspect experiments in your climb and compare scored runs through the hill's Leaderboard. Follow the organizers' separate submission and review instructions for competition credit.

A list curates problems. A hill fixes the task and evaluator. A climb records your team's attempt and experiments. A hill score measures its stated task; mathematical acceptance also requires the competition's formal artifact/certificate and review.

Research catalog

The following entries reuse the existing public catalog, including its statements, weekly scopes, and attribution. The 16 field labels follow that source. These descriptions are research leads; they are not 100 additional executable hills or an approved competition focus set. To pursue one, agree a precise target and verification method with the organizers before claiming score eligibility.

The OPDP v1.6 snapshot (assessed 2026-07-31) supplies the T5–T7 bands. Those bands are curation estimates for partial progress, not measured success probabilities or a promise of solving a full conjecture in a week. Each entry needs a current statement and literature check. In particular, entry 1's quasipolynomial graph-isomorphism question is already resolved; its correction appears below. Other entries retain the source snapshot and have not received a fresh complete status audit here.

Authorship and review

The username beside this list identifies its creator and curator, alejandrozu (Alejandro Zarzuelo Urdiales). Original problems retain the attribution in their linked sources. Teams retain authorship of their new work under the competition rules; creating this list does not transfer mathematical authorship to its curator.

For official rules and the release of the submission workspace and checker instructions, use OpenMath and its Competition and Judging Handbook. Ask the organizers about team setup and the competition-wide standings. Each AutoLab hill already has its own Leaderboard.
