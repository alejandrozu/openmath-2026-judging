A rank-23 scheme for 3x3 matrix multiplication with 138 nonzero coefficients

Chandragupt Sharma

2026-10-02

Abstract

We give an exact rational bilinear algorithm that multiplies two 3x3 matrices with 23 scalar multiplications, the same rank as Laderman's 1976 algorithm, using 138 nonzero coefficients in total across the three coefficient matrices U, V and W. The smallest previously published count at rank 23 is 139, from Smirnov's 2013 scheme, and every sparsity search published since then has stopped at 139. The new scheme is not equivalent to Smirnov's or to any published rank-23 scheme under the usual transformations. Its coefficients are, after a rescaling, 126 entries equal to ±1 and 12 entries equal to ±2 or ±1/2, so the practical cost is 83 additions and 12 bit shifts, against 84 additions for Smirnov's scheme and 98 for Laderman's. The scheme was found by a support-capped random walk over GF(3) with a fixed "core" planted, followed by a numerical lift of the resulting zero pattern over the reals, snapping to powers of two and an exact repair over the rationals. Its nonzero pattern is not a valid scheme over GF(2), which explains why the GF(2)-based methods that produced the published sparse schemes could not see it. The scheme was verified by two independent exact checkers and by the official evaluator of the search task. We also report the structure of the scheme (an exact order-2 symmetry, a one-parameter family that is an explicit rational curve, local optimality under single-entry removal), a classification of everything the walk finds at supports 139 to 142 (68 symmetry classes; at 140 and 141 every class is a known published scheme or a partial-sum rewrite of the 138 or of Smirnov's 139), a census of all 1,811 pairing cores, a second inequivalent scheme at support 143 on a different core, and the exact and empirical negative results around 137. Nothing here bears on tensor rank: rank 23 remains the best known for 3x3 and rank 22 remains open.

1. Introduction

A bilinear algorithm for 3x3 matrix multiplication with r multiplications writes each of the r products as a linear form in the entries of A times a linear form in the entries of B, and recovers each entry of C as a linear combination of the products. The number r is the rank of the scheme. Laderman gave a scheme with r = 23 in 1976, and no scheme with 22 multiplications has been found since; the best lower bound on the rank over the rationals is 19 (Blaser 2003), so the rank of 3x3 matrix multiplication is known only to lie between 19 and 23.

Beyond the rank, a scheme has a second cost: the number of additions it needs. In the naive accounting, each linear form costs one addition less than its number of nonzero coefficients, and each output entry costs one addition less than the number of products feeding it. For 23 products and 9 outputs this is the support (the number of nonzero coefficients in U, V and W together) minus 55. Laderman's scheme has support 153, or 98 additions. Smirnov's 2013 scheme, found by numerical optimisation with a sparsity-promoting model, has support 139, or 84 additions. Smirnov made no claim of minimality, but 139 has stood as the smallest published support at rank 23 since 2013: the Heule, Kauers and Seidl SAT collection of 17,376 schemes, the 15,316-scheme neural-network collection of 2026 (the "Hell's Bells" collection), Perminov's sparsity-directed flip graphs (best 143), the AlphaTensor and AlphaEvolve schemes (165 and 150) and Benson and Ballard's numerical schemes (142 and up) all have a sparsest member at 139 or above, and whenever a collection reaches 139 that member is Smirnov's scheme.

There is a structural reason for this. The published sparse schemes all have coefficients in {0, ±1}. A scheme with such coefficients reduces modulo 2 to a valid scheme over GF(2) with the same nonzero pattern, and conversely a GF(2) scheme can be lifted back to signs when a linear system over 0/1 has a solution. So every method that generates schemes over GF(2) (SAT, flip graphs, local search on the factor bits) or that reduces its candidates modulo 2 explores exactly the world of ±1 schemes. In that world we found, by exhaustive sweeps described in Section 8, that the bottom is rigid: exactly nine symmetry classes exist at support 141 or below (one at 139, two at 140, six at 141), every published collection bottoms out on the same nine, and no replacement of up to five of the 23 terms of the 139 can reach 138.

The scheme reported here lives outside that world. After the best rescaling it needs twelve coefficients equal to ±2 or ±1/2, and its nonzero pattern is not a valid scheme over GF(2): no GF(2)-based method can represent it, let alone find it. It was found by a support-capped walk over GF(3), where coefficients ±2 and ±1/2 are units and are visible.

The rest of the paper states the conventions and the claim precisely (Section 2), describes the method (Section 3) and the scheme (Section 4), gives the verification (Section 5), proves the inequivalence from published schemes (Section 6), maps the landscape around the scheme (Section 7), records what was tried and did not work (Section 8), and states the limitations (Section 9), the reproduction procedure (Section 10) and the disclosures (Section 11).

2. Conventions and the claim

A scheme of rank r is three r x 9 matrices U, V, W of exact rational numbers. The rows of U and V index the entries of A and B row-major (entry (i, j) at index 3i + j). The rows of W index the entries of C as 3 * column + row. With this indexing the scheme is correct if and only if the 729 Brent identities hold: for all a, b, c in 0..8,

sum over t = 1..r of U[t][a] V[t][b] W[t][c] = T[a][b][c],

where T[3i + j][3j + k][3k + i] = 1 for all i, j, k in 0..2 and every other entry of T is 0. In tensor form this is the trace tensor T[(i, j), (j, k), (k, i)] = 1 with every slot row-major, which is the convention of src/brent.py and of the official evaluator. The support of a scheme is the number of nonzero entries of U, V and W together. The naive addition count of a scheme is the sum over terms of (nnz(u) - 1) + (nnz(v) - 1) plus the sum over the 9 outputs of (number of contributing terms - 1); for every scheme in this paper it equals support - 55.

Two schemes are equivalent if one is carried onto the other by the transformations that preserve validity and support: a sandwich A -> P A Q^-1, B -> Q B R^-1, C -> R C P^-1 with permutation matrices P, Q, R; the cyclic shift of the three slots (u, v, w) -> (v, w, u); the transpose (u, v, w) -> (w^T, v^T, u^T); reordering of the terms; and rescaling of a term (u a, v b, w / (a b)). Permutation sandwiches and slot maps give 1,296 maps; "gauge" means term rescaling combined with a diagonal sandwich, which changes coefficient values but not the nonzero pattern.

The claim. We give a scheme of rank 23 and support 138, exact over the rationals, that passes all 729 Brent identities. This reduces the smallest known support at rank 23 from 139 (Smirnov 2013) to 138. The scheme is not equivalent, under the maps above, to Smirnov's scheme or to any scheme in the collection of 17,387 verified published rank-23 schemes, and a literature search found no published or posted scheme with support 138 or less (Section 6).

What is not claimed. No reduction of tensor rank is claimed: rank 23 is the best known for 3x3 and rank 22 remains open, and nothing in this work bears on it. Support 138 is not proven minimal; the evidence against 137 is empirical (Section 8).

Practical cost. As submitted, the scheme has coefficients ±2^k with k in -4..4. After the optimal rescaling (an exact mixed-integer optimum, Section 4), 126 coefficients are ±1 and 12 are ±2 or ±1/2, which are bit shifts. The naive addition count is 83, against 84 for Smirnov's 139 and 98 for Laderman's 153. In float64 the rescaled form is as accurate as Smirnov's scheme and Laderman's: on 10,000 random trials with entries uniform in [-1, 1] the worst relative error was 1.68e-15 for the rescaled 138, 1.50e-15 for the 139 and 2.12e-15 for Laderman (the mean errors were 2.99e-16, 2.45e-16 and 2.89e-16; numpy's own product gave a worst error of 4.1e-16 on the same trials). The stored, unrescaled form is about 7 times less accurate (worst error 1.09e-14) and should not be used for floating-point work.

3. Method

The method has four steps: a support-capped random walk over GF(3) with a planted core, which produces a nonzero pattern; a numerical lift of that pattern over the reals; a snap of the lifted coefficients to powers of two; and an exact repair over the rationals. Only the pattern comes from the walk; the exact coefficients come from the lift.

Step 1: the capped walk over GF(3). The walker (walk3.c) is a GF(3) version of the "semantic" local search on the factor bits that Palladinos used over GF(2) (arXiv 2607.29291). Its state is the 23 x 3 x 9 array of factor entries with values in {0, 1, 2}; a move changes one entry; the score is the number of the 729 Brent identities that are violated modulo 3. Two things were added to the published walk. First, a core is planted: the 81 factor entries that produce the 27 needed products a_ij b_jk c_ki in Smirnov's scheme (four terms producing two needed products each and nineteen producing one) are fixed to 1 and never moved. Second, a hard cap on the support is enforced: the walk never accepts a state with more than 138 nonzero entries. The walk therefore moves through invalid schemes and only stops when the residual reaches 0 at or below the cap. Each seed runs 400,000 "break" steps followed by 600,000 "break-minus-make" steps at temperature 1.5, from an initial density of 1/12. Calibration with Smirnov's core planted: at cap 139 the walk rediscovers the 139 in 6 of 30 seeds; at cap 141 it also finds schemes at 141 whose pattern is not a GF(2) scheme.

Why GF(3) sees what GF(2) cannot. A rational scheme whose coefficients are 3-adic units (±1, ±2, ±1/2, ±4, ±1/4, and so on) reduces modulo 3 to a valid GF(3) scheme with the same support. A scheme with a coefficient 2 or 1/2 has no such reduction modulo 2 that preserves its pattern, so the GF(2) world contains only the ±1 schemes. Earlier GF(3) flip walks (about 2.8 billion flips, moving only through valid schemes) had floored at 139 and had seen patterns needing 1/2 or 2 only at support 141 and above; what changed was the planted core and the hard cap, which let the walk pass through infeasible states. Coefficients divisible by 3 are not visible modulo 3; the GF(5) search of Section 7 covers that gap partially.

The hit. Seed 3158 at cap 138 reached residual 0 with 138 nonzero entries (47 in U, 47 in V, 44 in W) after 447,940 steps: a valid GF(3) scheme of rank 23 whose nonzero pattern is not a valid GF(2) scheme (29 entries equal to 2). It was the only hit in 316 seeds; cap 137 gave 0 hits in 300 seeds on the same day. The walk is deterministic: the seed, the core file and the parameters reproduce the pattern byte for byte (Section 10).

Step 2: numerical lift. With the zero pattern fixed and the 138 nonzero entries free, the 729 Brent identities were solved by least squares (Levenberg-Marquardt) from 64 random starts. Several starts reached residual 5e-15, so the pattern is feasible over the reals.

Step 3: gauge fix and snap. The term rescaling gauge was fixed by setting the first nonzero of U and of V in each term to 1. The remaining solution set was a 7-parameter family (Jacobian rank 85 of 92). The free parameters were then snapped, one at a time, to the grid {0, ±1, ±1/2, ±2} as long as a solution survived.

Step 4: exact repair. U and V were rounded to rationals, and W was solved exactly over the rationals from the identities, which are linear in W once U and V are fixed. The result was an exact scheme with 138 nonzero coefficients, all of the form ±2^k with k in -4..4, which passed all 729 identities. It was saved as solutions/support_138/solution.json and submitted on 2026-09-30.

4. The scheme

The committed file stores the scheme in the gauge in which the lift produced it, with 73 coefficients not equal to ±1 (1: 60, -1: 5, ±2: 12, ±4: 3, ±8: 22, ±16: 6, ±1/2: 6, ±1/4: 6, ±1/8: 14, ±1/16: 4). The gauge is not a structural property. An exact mixed-integer optimisation over term rescalings and diagonal sandwiches found the minimum number of non-±1 coefficients to be 12, and the count stays 12 even when real (non-dyadic) diagonal scalings are allowed. The rescaled form, solutions/support_138_gauged/solution.json, was verified independently (0 failing identities, support 138) and is the form to use in practice. Its twelve non-±1 entries are u[1][5] = 1/2, u[2][5] = -1/2, u[3][5] = -1/2, u[8][0] = 2, u[12][5] = 1/2, u[14][5] = -1/2, v[1][7] = -2, v[4][7] = -2, v[13][6] = -2, v[15][2] = 1/2, v[15][5] = -1/2 and w[14][1] = 2.

All 81 planted entries of Smirnov's core are nonzero in the 138, and 44 of them equal 1. In both the 138 and the 139, four terms produce two needed products each and nineteen produce one. The 138 breaks the "perfect pairing" of the 139, in which every unwanted product appears in exactly two terms and cancels: in the 138, four unwanted products are each covered by three terms, and they cancel through coefficients outside ±1. This is the combinatorial reason the pattern is not a GF(2) scheme.

4.2 The exact order-2 symmetry

The 138 has an exact symmetry of order 2. The map is the slot transform (rotation by 2 followed by transpose) with permutations P = (1, 2, 0), Q = (1, 0, 2), R = (2, 0, 1), followed by the diagonal sandwich P = diag(-1, 16, 8), Q = diag(1, 1, 4), R = diag(-1, -1/2, 1/16). Its image equals the 138 as a set of rank-1 tensors, checked exactly. It fixes terms 9, 14 and 21 and swaps the pairs (0, 3), (1, 2), (4, 20), (5, 12), (6, 17), (7, 15), (8, 22), (10, 13), (11, 18) and (16, 19). Smirnov's 139 has a symmetry of the same shape on its zero pattern only; no choice of signs or diagonal scalings makes it exact.

4.3 The one-parameter family

The exact Jacobian of the 729 identities with respect to the nonzero coefficients was computed over the rationals, with the first nonzero of U and of V in each term pinned. For the 138 there are 92 free coefficients, the Jacobian has rank 85, and the null space has dimension 7: six directions are the diagonal-sandwich gauge and one is genuine. (Smirnov's 139 has 93 free coefficients, rank 84 and three genuine directions; Laderman has 107, rank 95 and six.)

Pinning u[0][4], u[1][5], u[2][5], u[2][8], v[0][4] and v[1][6] of the stored 138 removes the diagonal gauge. What remains is a single curve, parametrised by s = u[12][6], with the 138 at s = -1. The curve was reconstructed from 60 exact rational points at s = -k/32 by rational interpolation and then checked on all 729 identities at 22 fresh rational values of s; every degree involved is at most 2, so the identities hold identically in s. The 96 other coefficients are constant along the curve. The 42 moving coefficients, in the stored frame, are:

u: u[3][6] = -8(s+2), u[7][1] = s/(8(s+2)), u[7][2] = 1/(4(s+2)), u[9][8] = -8s, u[10][4] = s/(8(s+2)), u[10][5] = 1/(4(s+2)), u[11][6] = -8s, u[12][6] = s, u[18][7] = -8s, u[20][7] = s/(8(s+2)), u[20][8] = 1/(4(s+2)).
v: v[0][6] = -4(s+2), v[1][7] = -8/(s+2), v[2][6] = s/(4(s+2)), v[3][5] = 16(s+2)^2/s, v[5][6] = -s/2, v[9][8] = v[11][2] = v[12][2] = v[18][5] = 2(s+2), v[14][6] = -4(s+2), v[17][5] = -8(s+2)/s, v[22][8] = -s/2.
w: w[1][4] = -(s+2)/8, w[2][2] = (s+2)/(4s), w[3][2] = -1/(8(s+2)), w[3][7] = s/(16(s+2)^2), w[5][2] = 1/(8s), w[7][0] = 4(s+2), w[8][6] = 8/(s+2), w[9][6] = 1/(2(s+2)), w[10][7] = -1/(2(s+2)), w[12][7] = 1/(2(s+2)), w[14][1] = 1/(4(s+2)), w[14][2] = 1/(8(s+2)), w[16][4] = w[17][7] = -s, w[18][2] = -1/(8s), w[20][2] = -16(s+2)^2/s, w[20][8] = 8(s+2)/s, w[21][6] = -8s/(s+2), w[22][8] = -2/s.

For every s other than 0, -2 and infinity all 138 coefficients are nonzero, so no point of the curve has support 137. At s = -1 every coefficient is a power of 2; other rational values of s bring in denominators such as 3, 5 and 7. At the three ends s -> 0, -2 and infinity, the best combined diagonal and term gauge still leaves some coefficient growing like |s - s0|^(-1/6), so all three ends are border-rank-type degenerations, not schemes.

4.4 Local optimality

For each of the 138 nonzero entries, that entry was set to 0, the other 137 were left free, everything outside the pattern was held at 0, and the 729 identities were solved by least squares from 40 starts (8 near the family and 32 random). No entry reached a residual below 1e-6. For 81 entries the best residual was 1.000 (one identity cannot be satisfied); for the other 57 it lay between 1.21e-5 and 1.86e-3 and was still falling when the runs stopped. Two of these were followed up with 8,000 and 15,000 extra iterations: the residuals fell to 4.38e-6 and 3.30e-6 while the largest coefficient grew, with log-log slopes of -0.17 and -0.166 of the largest coefficient against the residual. Both slopes match the exact exponent -1/6 at the ends of the family, so these are border-type approaches, not schemes. Forcing a whole term to zero (a rank-22 test on this pattern) gave best residuals of 1.000 for terms 4 to 22 and 1.414 for terms 0 to 3, with no trend towards 0.

So, within its family and under single-entry removal, the 138 is locally optimal. This is a numerical statement (least squares from 40 starts per entry), not an exact proof; the exact statements are the verification itself, the curve of Section 4.3 and the invariants of Section 6.

5. Verification

The scheme was checked in four independent ways.

verify/verify.py: a dependency-free Python checker using exact rational arithmetic (fractions.Fraction). It checks every scheme under solutions/ against all 729 Brent identities and reports rank and support. It can also export a scheme for the second checker, with each of U, V and W scaled to integers by the least common multiple of its denominators.
verify/verify.c: a dependency-free C checker using integer arithmetic only, on the exported integer form; it checks the identities with the target scaled by the product of the three scale factors.
src/brent.py: the research code's own exact checker (brent_errors, which counts failing identities exactly), used throughout the search; the 138, the 139 and Laderman's scheme all give 0.
The official evaluator of the AutoLab hill alejandrozu/matrix-multiplication-tensor-3x3 (hill tree 526770e74b465dbd444049d169e2f373eeb0339d). The submission was checked as experiment b8e0716a on 2026-09-30 (timestamp 2026-09-30T05:18:26Z): PASSED, rank 23, support 138, coefficient domain Q, 729 Brent identities checked and 2 private replays checked. The evaluator never runs submitted code; it expands the scheme exactly and checks the identities and hidden matrix-product tests. The signed report is evaluation/evaluator_report_b8e0716a.txt, with a leaderboard snapshot next to it.

The same checks were applied to the rescaled form (solutions/support_138_gauged), to the exotic 139 (solutions/exotic_139_support_139) and to the core-1809 scheme at 143 (solutions/core1809_support_143) described below.

A Lean 4 certificate (directory lean/, core Lean 4 v4.34.1, no Mathlib, toolchain pinned) proves by decide that the integer-scaled schemes satisfy all 729 Brent identities and have the stated supports: twelve theorems for the 138, the gauged 138, the exotic 139 and the core-1809 143, building in about 15 seconds; a deliberately corrupted copy fails to build.

6. Comparison with earlier schemes and inequivalence

An invariant. The multiset of factor-rank triples of the 23 terms is preserved by every map of Section 2 (sandwiches, slot shifts, transposes, term order and rescaling), so it separates inequivalent schemes. The 138 has 15 terms of type (1,1,1), 4 of type (1,1,2) and 4 of type (2,2,2). Smirnov's 139 has 11, 8 and 4; Laderman's scheme has 13 of type (1,1,1), 6 of type (1,1,3) and 4 of type (2,2,2). The 138 is therefore equivalent to neither.

Alignment with Smirnov's 139. As stored, the two schemes share no identical rank-1 term and no term with proportional factors. Exactly 2 of the 1,296 maps send the core of the 139 onto the core of the 138. In that frame, with one signed power-of-2 diagonal sandwich, terms 8, 9, 21 and 22 of the two schemes are identical and the other 19 terms have different zero patterns; in total 20 entries appear only in the 138 and 21 only in the 139 (pattern symmetric difference 41). A greedy search over all 1,296 maps plus a free signed diagonal finds at most 4 equal terms. The 138 is thus not a small edit of the 139 but a 19-term rewrite of it on the same core.

The published collections. 17,387 published rank-23 schemes were downloaded and verified exactly: the 17,376 schemes of the Heule, Kauers and Seidl collection, 10 from Perminov's database and 1 from AlphaTensor. Since each is published in one arbitrary form, the sparsest equivalent form of every one of the 17,376 Heule, Kauers and Seidl schemes was computed by an exhaustive sweep over all 168^3 = 4.74 million GF(2) basis changes. The minimum is 139, and the classes with a sparsest form at 141 or below are exactly nine: one at 139 (Smirnov's), two at 140 and six at 141. The same sweep over the independent 15,316-scheme Hell's Bells collection gave minimum 139 (235 schemes, all Smirnov's class), the same two classes at 140 and the same six at 141. The 138 is in neither collection: its pattern is not a GF(2) scheme, so it cannot be.

The literature. Two searches on 2026-09-30 covered arXiv listings through 2026-09-29, Google Scholar, GitHub, Zenodo, IACR ePrint, MathOverflow, Sedoglavic's catalogue, the Linz collection page, Smirnov's publication list and the 82 works citing Smirnov 2013, with exact phrases in English, Russian and Chinese, and then the older literature (Laderman 1976, Sykora 1977, Johnson and McLoughlin 1986, Makarov 1986, Pan 1984, Blaser 2003, Courtois et al. 2011, Drevet et al. 2011, Oh et al. 2013, Benson and Ballard 2015, Karstadt and Schwartz 2020, Beniamini et al. 2019 and 2020). No source states a rank-23 3x3 scheme with 138 or fewer nonzero coefficients or 83 or fewer naive additions. Every published naive count bottoms out at 84 (Smirnov 2013, who publishes one 3x3 scheme and makes no minimality claim). Lower figures in print (62, 61, 59, 55, 52, 51 and so on) are straight-line counts after common-subexpression elimination or counts in an alternative basis, not nonzero counts. No published work combines a planted core with a support cap, runs a capped search over a non-binary field, or explores the power-of-2 coefficient class for sparsity. The one earlier rational-coefficient source, Johnson and McLoughlin 1986, gives two families with entries such as 1/2, 1/3, 3/2, 2 and 3, but no nonzero count was ever published for them.

What the 138 changes structurally. Every published scheme at support 141 or below has Smirnov's core and the perfect-pairing shape (each needed product made once, each unwanted product cancelled in exactly two terms). The 138 keeps the core and the pairing of needed products but breaks the perfect pairing: four unwanted products are cancelled across three terms each, which is possible only with coefficients outside ±1.

7. The landscape around the scheme

After the find, the walker was run at scale on free notebook compute in seven waves. Everything the walk produced was deduplicated exactly, classified up to the 1,296 maps, the 64 signed diagonal sandwiches, term order and per-term scaling, compared with the published schemes, and in the relevant cases lifted to exact rational schemes.

Without the planted core the walk reached neither 138 nor 141 in 57,112 seeds, so the core is essential at this budget (1 million steps per seed). The exotic 139 is the 138 in disguise. Both exotic classes lift to exact rational schemes (0 failing identities, support 139, 16 non-±1 coefficients, denominators 1 and 2 only), and they are one scheme up to symmetry: the 138's order-2 symmetry maps one onto the other. In the 138's own frame the exotic 139 shares 21 of 23 terms with the 138 exactly; the other two terms on each side share the factor v = E_{3,2}, sum to the same rank-2 tensor, and differ only by a GL(2) change of basis on that pair. Smirnov's 139 is unrelated to it (44 pattern entries away). The exotic 139 is kept as solutions/exotic_139_support_139.

Three findings follow.

Nothing new in the GF(2) world at 140 and 141. Every GF(2)-valid class at 140 has the mod-2 pattern of one of the two known published 140s, and every one at 141 of one of five of the six known 141s (the walk never reached the sixth). This agrees with the exact GF(2) sweeps of Section 8. As a side finding, one GF(2)-valid pattern at 142 (62 hits) is absent from the 17,387-scheme collection, which lists exactly 7 patterns at 142.
No new mod-2-invisible family at 140 or 141. Of the 13 invisible classes there, 8 are rewrites of the 138 on one group of two or three terms that share a factor, 4 are rewrites of Smirnov's 139, and the last (4 hits) is the 138 rewritten in two places, as its exact lift showed. Every invisible class at 139 to 141 differs from the 138 or from Smirnov's 139 on at most 5 terms.
One construction generates nearly all of it. Take two terms that share a factor; their sum is (shared factor) tensor M with M of rank 2, and any 2x2 change of basis on the pair gives another valid scheme. Applied to the exact 138, this reproduces, exactly and with dyadic coefficients, the exotic 139, two of the three invisible 140 classes, three of the invisible 141 classes and two classes at 142. Applied to Smirnov's 139, it reproduces both known published 140s, four of the known 141s, all four high-volume invisible 141 classes (7,968 hits between them, 40 percent of all hits at 141) and four classes at 142. The remaining 138-rewrites use three-term groups and rest on exact numeric lifts. Exact family dimensions (6 gauge directions removed): the 138 and its one-pair rewrites have dimension 1 to 2; Smirnov's 139, the known 140s and their rewrites 3 to 4.

Reading: below 143, with Smirnov's core planted, the GF(3) walk sees exactly two sources, the 138's family and Smirnov's family, and everything else is a partial-sum rewrite of one of them. "Invisible mod 2" at these levels means a pair rewrite with ±2^k coefficients, not new combinatorics. The 138 remains the unique known point below 139.

7.3 The census of all 1,811 cores

A core records which needed products share a term. For cores of the type of every known scheme at 146 or below (four index-disjoint pairs and nineteen singles) there are 2,186,757 pairings, which fall into 1,811 classes under the 1,296 maps. In waves 5 and 5b every one of the 1,811 cores was planted (all 81 entries of its needed products fixed to 1 over GF(3)) and walked with 40 seeds at cap 144, temperature 2.5 and 1 million steps per seed, with Smirnov's core (index 1808) as a positive control in each of the 15 notebooks of wave 5b.

Control: Smirnov's core solved in all 15 control notebooks, in 14 to 27 of 40 seeds each, with minimum supports 139 to 142.
The other 1,810 cores: exactly one solved, core 1809, in 2 of 40 seeds, both at support 143 with mod-2-invisible patterns. Core 1809 is not in the symmetry orbit of Smirnov's core (the 1,811 cores are pairwise inequivalent; checked). Its stabiliser in the 1,296 maps has order 6 (Smirnov's has order 2).
Nearest misses: core 1685 reached residual 1 (one violated identity); cores 1627, 1701, 1729, 1732 and 1741 reached residual 2; 29 more reached 3; the remaining 1,774 cores never got below 4 in 40 seeds.

An earlier census of the same 1,811 cores over GF(2) (cap 145, 30 seeds per core, then 200 seeds on the 64 nearest cores) had found schemes for only four cores, all of them already published (Smirnov's with minimum 139, and three others at 142, 143 and 144), and nothing at cap 138 on any of them. The reading of both censuses is that the pairing structure of the needed products is the decisive choice: over GF(3), with this walker and budget, only 2 of the 1,811 cores produced any rank-23 scheme at support 144 or below and only Smirnov's reached below 143; over GF(2), three further cores responded at 142 to 144, all already published; and in both censuses only Smirnov's core gets below 142.

7.4 Core 1809: a new scheme at support 143

The two census hits on core 1809 are one scheme: the second is the image of the first under one of the six symmetries of core 1809. The exact lift succeeded (8 of 64 starts converged, best residual 1.24e-15, every parameter snapped to the dyadic grid, exact repair): 0 failing identities, support 143, 35 non-±1 coefficients, denominators 1 and 2 only (1: 86, -1: 22, 1/2: 10, -1/2: 14, 2: 6, -2: 5), re-verified independently in both frames. Its exact family has dimension 1, like the 138.

It is new. Its mod-2 pattern matches none of the 137 known scheme files, including the 14 published schemes at support 143, under any of the 1,296 maps. It is not a rewrite of anything on Smirnov's core: the best alignment shares only 4 of 23 terms with the 138 (pattern difference 43) and 2 with Smirnov's 139 (68); the nearest published scheme shares 4 terms (difference 42); the exact term-level test confirms 4 of 23 terms equal as tensors versus the 138, all of them single-product terms. It has the same skeleton as the 138 and the 139 (four pair-terms, nineteen single-product terms, every needed product made by exactly one term) but 78 distinct unwanted products with 162 incidences (72 of multiplicity 2 and 6 of multiplicity 3), against 69 with 142 incidences for the 138 and 68 with 136 for the 139; that is where its five extra nonzeros go. Its pattern has a trivial automorphism group.

It is locally optimal in the same sense as the 138: forcing any one of its 143 nonzeros to zero and re-solving from 40 starts never reached a residual below 8.5e-7 (the 81 needed-product entries gave residual 1, the 62 cancellation entries 8.5e-7 to 1.6e-3), the two smallest are border-type limits, and along its exact one-parameter family no entry vanishes at a finite point. Wave 7 walked core 1809 at caps 140, 141 and 142 (82,744 seeds): no hit, so this family floors at 143 at this budget. The scheme is kept as solutions/core1809_support_143.

7.5 GF(5) sees the same objects

A GF(5) walker (walk5.c) was run in wave 6 with Smirnov's core planted as nonzero but free, for 139,888 seeds. Modulo 5 every coefficient whose denominator is not a multiple of 5 is visible, in particular denominators 3 that modulo 3 cannot see. At cap 139 (29,544 control seeds) it produced 2,279 hits at 139 and 5 at 138; at cap 138 (110,344 seeds) it produced 4 hits at 138. All 9 GF(5) hits at support 138 have the known 138's nonzero pattern (checked against its 648-pattern symmetry orbit) and differ only by the diagonal gauge. The 2,279 hits at 139 are all Smirnov's pattern except one, which is the exotic 139. The GF(5) hit rate at 138 was about 1 per 27,000 seeds, against 1 per 105 for GF(3) at temperature 2.5. Nothing with a denominator 3 appeared at 138 or 139: there is no evidence of a sub-139 scheme that needs a 3 in a denominator.

8. What was tried and did not work

The search ran from 2026-09-27 to 2026-10-02. The negative results fall into the exact GF(2) sweeps, which are proofs within their stated scope, and the empirical searches, which are evidence only.

8.1 The route to 139

Our own numerical search (Levenberg-Marquardt with an imposed cyclic symmetry, a shrinking penalty on coefficient size to reject border-rank solutions, reweighted-L1 sparsification and an exact rational repair of W) found rank-23 schemes from random starts and reached support 147 (an attractor hit 27 times in 120 starts), but never lower; it also rediscovered Laderman's scheme from scratch. Its rank-22 runs found no exact scheme in 120 starts, and the near-misses were shown to be border-rank artifacts whose error shrinks only as the coefficients grow. A sparsity-biased flip-graph walk over GF(2) (Kauers and Moosbauer's flip graph with a push towards fewer nonzeros, and sign lifting by a linear system modulo 4) reached 141 and then 139 in 25 seconds on the laptop; the 139 is Smirnov's scheme modulo 2. About 27 billion further flips reached only nine distinct schemes at support 141 or below.

8.3 Other searches that stayed at 139

Flip walks with the nine known low schemes banned (14.8 billion flips: only new 142s); flip walks seeded from about 160 published schemes (23.5 billion flips: nothing new at 141 or below); an alternating exact descent that fixes one factor table and SAT-solves the other two, from 647 published seeds (every run ended at Smirnov's 139 or one of two known 141s); GF(3) flip walks moving only through valid schemes (about 2.8 billion flips: minimum 139, patterns needing 1/2 or 2 only at 141 and above); a rational pattern-exchange search around the nine known classes (179 candidate patterns proven infeasible, 17 numerically unsolved, only the nine known schemes reached); seeds from restricting 875 published larger-format schemes to 3x3x3 (26 million restrictions, 31,913 of rank 23, 1,261 classes of which 883 were new: minimum 139, new classes only from 144 up); and the capped walk over GF(2) with Smirnov's core planted at cap 138 (0 of 200 seeds) and on the 13 best published cores (only Smirnov's gets below 142).

8.4 The search for 137 (empirical)
GF(3), Smirnov's core planted, cap 137: 0 hits in 416,104 seeds, in wave 1 (185,192 seeds at the default setting) and wave 4 (230,912 seeds across three further walker settings of initial density and temperature; each setting had a cap-138 control that re-found the 138, at rates from 1 per 2,250 seeds to 1 per 105). Under the best setting a 137 reachable at the 138's rate would have produced about 870 hits in its 91,400 seeds; none appeared.
GF(5), cap 138: 110,344 seeds found only the known 138 (Section 7.5).
Every other core: the GF(3) census of Section 7.3 found nothing at 144 or below on 1,809 of the 1,811 cores, and 143 on core 1809; the GF(2) census found nothing at cap 138 on the four responding cores and the eleven nearest misses (200 seeds each).
The 138's own family: no point of the curve of Section 4.3 and no single-entry removal (Section 4.4) gives 137.
Variants of the core: the 27 one-unplanted-cell variants of Smirnov's core at cap 138, 100 seeds each, gave nothing.

These are empirical results: the walk is a heuristic, and a 137 that the walk cannot reach from these cores, or that needs coefficients invisible to both GF(3) and GF(5), is not excluded.

9. Limitations
138 is not proven minimal. The evidence against a 137 reachable by our method is empirical: no hit in 416,104 GF(3) walks with several parameter settings, 110,344 GF(5) walks at cap 138, and a census of all 1,811 index-disjoint cores over GF(3) in which only two cores produce any scheme at support 144 or below. Cores with a pair of needed products sharing an index, and cores with groups of three or four products, were not covered.
Local optimality of the 138 (no single coefficient can be removed) is a numerical result (least squares from 40 starts per entry), not an exact proof. The exact statements are the verification itself, the explicit curve and the inequivalence invariant.
The exact GF(2) sweeps prove nonexistence only within their stated neighbourhoods and coefficient class ({0, ±1}).
Nothing here bears on tensor rank 22.
The Lean 4 certificate (directory lean/, core Lean 4 v4.34.1, no Mathlib, toolchain pinned) proves by decide that the integer-scaled schemes satisfy all 729 Brent identities and have the stated supports; it proves nothing about minimality, about the search, or about rank 22. Verification is otherwise by exact rational arithmetic in two independent dependency-free programs, by the research code's checker and by the official evaluator.
10. Reproducibility

Verifying the scheme. The scheme is solutions/support_138/solution.json (coefficients ±2^k, k in -4..4, as submitted) and solutions/support_138_gauged/solution.json (the same scheme after rescaling, 126 entries ±1 and 12 entries ±2 or ±1/2). Either file is a JSON object with keys u, v, w, each a list of 23 rows of 9 entries; an entry is an integer or a pair [numerator, denominator]. From the repository root:

python3 verify/verify.py checks every scheme under solutions/ in exact rational arithmetic.
python3 verify/verify.py solutions/support_138_gauged --export-int scheme_int.txt exports the integer form.
gcc -O2 -o verify_c verify/verify.c && ./verify_c scheme_int.txt checks it with integer arithmetic only.

Both checkers check all 729 Brent identities in the convention of Section 2.

Reproducing the find. The walk is deterministic: seed 3158 of walk3 on Smirnov's core at cap 138 (parameters: initial density 1/12, 400,000 break steps, 600,000 gain steps, temperature 1.5) produces the pattern, and the lift with fixed seeds produces an exact scheme with the same pattern. bash reproduce/reproduce.sh recreates the scheme from walker seed 3158 in about 12 seconds (walk, numerical lift with fixed seeds, snap, exact repair, verification); three runs gave byte-identical output, and reproduce/README.md lists the expected output

Pinned versions. Every result in the repository was produced with Python 3.14.7, numpy 2.5.3, scipy 1.18.1 and python-flint 0.9.0 on Linux x86-64 with gcc 16.2.1 (reproduce/requirements.txt). Only numpy, scipy and python-flint are needed to verify and to reproduce the scheme; python-sat (1.9.dev15; any version at or above 1.8 works) is used only by the SAT tools under src/ and results/.

Layout. src/ holds the research code of the whole event (exact checker, flip-graph and tabu walkers over GF(2) and GF(3), SAT neighbourhood tools, numerical search with exact repair); results/approaches.md is the full log of what was tried, with every negative result. solutions/ holds our schemes. kaggle/ holds the batch search run after the find: the runner, every wave's configuration, per-notebook summaries, compressed raw hits and logs, and the analysis; kaggle/analysis/REPORT_138_structure.md (sections A to M) with the reports under kaggle/analysis/lift139/, census140/ and core1809/ is the detailed account behind Sections 4 and 7. results/ holds the collections of published schemes used for comparison (17,387 verified), the literature notes and the artifacts of the exact GF(2) sweeps. evaluation/ holds the official evaluator report, the submission identifiers and the leaderboard snapshot.
